# The simplified likelihood

SGHF replaces a nonlinear HistFactory expectation and its auxiliary
constraints by a local linear response model with a quadratic fitting
objective. This makes fitting a constrained weighted least-squares problem.
It is an approximation to the original workspace likelihood, not a different
implementation of that likelihood.

This page defines the statistical model. The exact translation from workspace
objects into its arrays is given in [workspace compilation](workspace-compilation.md).
The construction is motivated by the
[HistFactory and pyhf model](references.md#histfactory-and-pyhf) and the
[profile-likelihood literature](references.md#profile-likelihood-and-asymptotic-tests).

## Operational summary

Loading a workspace produces one `Model` containing:

- a flattened nominal expectation $\boldsymbol{\nu}_0$;
- a response matrix $A$;
- an expansion point $\boldsymbol{\theta}_0$;
- parameter bounds and fixed-parameter flags;
- Gaussian auxiliary observations $\mathbf a$ and their diagonal precision
  $P$;
- the flattened observations $\mathbf n$.

The expected main measurement is

$$
\boldsymbol{\nu}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
=
\boldsymbol{\nu}_0
+
A(\boldsymbol{\theta}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_0).
$$

`Model.expected(parameters)` evaluates this expression. It raises
`ModelDomainError` if any expected yield is non-finite or non-positive.

There are two variance choices for fitting and inference:

| Variance | Definition | Main use |
|---|---|---|
| `nominal` | $V=\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\nu}_0)$ | One exact quadratic solve for the compiled model; the default for vectorized toy inference |
| `iterated` | $V$ is repeatedly updated from the current fitted expectation | Partially adapts the Gaussian variance to the fitted rate; the default for fits and asymptotic inference |

Neither choice restores the nonlinear HistFactory response. The iterated
choice changes only the diagonal weights in the quadratic objective.

## Notation and dimensions

Let $N$ be the number of flattened channel bins and $K$ the number of scalar
parameters after vector modifiers are expanded.

| Symbol | Dimension | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| $\mathbf n$ | $N$ | observed main-measurement bin contents |
| $\boldsymbol{\nu}_0$ | $N$ | expected yields at the expansion point |
| $A$ | $N\times K$ | local response; $A_{ij}=\partial\nu_i/\partial\theta_j$ at the expansion point as compiled by SGHF |
| $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ | $K$ | model parameters, including the parameter of interest |
| $\boldsymbol{\theta}_0$ | $K$ | configured initial values and response expansion point |
| $\mathbf a$ | $K$ | Gaussian auxiliary observations; entries with zero precision have no effect |
| $P$ | $K\times K$ | diagonal auxiliary-constraint precision |
| $V$ | $N\times N$ | diagonal main-measurement variance |
| $\mu$ | scalar | parameter of interest |

The free fit is denoted by $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$. A conditional fit with
$\mu$ fixed is denoted by
$\hat{\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(\mu)$.

## From a binned likelihood to a quadratic objective

For independent Poisson bins, the exact main-measurement likelihood has

$$
n_i\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\nu_i(\boldsymbol{\theta})).
$$

SGHF first replaces the nonlinear expectation by its compiled first-order
response:

$$
\nu_i(\boldsymbol{\theta})
\approx
\nu_{0,i}
+
\sum_{j=1}^{K}A_{ij}(\theta_j-\theta_{0,j}).
$$

It then uses a diagonal Gaussian approximation for the fitting objective:

$$
\mathbf n
\ \dot\sim\
N\!\left(
\boldsymbol{\nu}(\boldsymbol{\theta}), V
\right).
$$

The dot over $\sim$ emphasizes that this is the objective SGHF uses, not a
claim that the supplied data were generated by a Gaussian process.

All supported auxiliary constraints are represented as independent Gaussian
terms:

$$
\mathbf a
\ \dot\sim\
N(\boldsymbol{\theta},P^{-1})
$$

on the subspace where $P_{jj}>0$. A zero precision removes that parameter's
auxiliary penalty; $P^{-1}$ is therefore only notation for the constrained
subspace, not a required inverse of the full matrix.

After dropping additive constants, the objective is

$$
\boxed{
\chi^2(\boldsymbol{\theta})
=
(\mathbf n-\boldsymbol{\nu}(\boldsymbol{\theta}))^\mathsf T
V^{-1}
(\mathbf n-\boldsymbol{\nu}(\boldsymbol{\theta}))
+
(\boldsymbol{\theta}-\mathbf a)^\mathsf T
P
(\boldsymbol{\theta}-\mathbf a)
}.
$$

With `variance="nominal"`,

$$
V=\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\nu}_0),
$$

so the response, weights, and penalties are parameter independent. The
objective is a quadratic polynomial in $\boldsymbol{\theta}$.

With `variance="iterated"`, iteration $k$ solves the same quadratic problem
with

$$
V^{(k)}
=
\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{v}^{(k)}),
$$

then updates $\boldsymbol{v}^{(k)}$ toward the positive fitted expectation.
The precise convergence and damping rules are documented in
[fitting](fitting.md).

## What is exact within the compiled model

For fixed $V$, no numerical approximation is made when forming the
unconstrained normal equations: the minimizer of the quadratic objective is a
weighted least-squares solution. Bounds can make the solution a constrained
least-squares problem, but the objective remains quadratic.

The following statements are therefore distinct:

1. The nominal solve is exact for the SGHF quadratic objective when the stated
   bounds are included.
2. The SGHF objective is only a local approximation to the original
   HistFactory likelihood.
3. A toy ensemble may use Poisson pseudo-observations even though each toy is
   evaluated with the SGHF quadratic statistic.

The vectorized nominal toy estimator additionally ignores parameter bounds;
its exact scope is stated in [toy inference](toy-inference.md).

## Approximations and their conditions

### 1. First-order parameter response

SGHF retains the value and first derivative of each supported response at
$\boldsymbol{\theta}_0$:

$$
\boldsymbol{\nu}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_0+\boldsymbol{\delta})
\approx
\boldsymbol{\nu}_0+A\boldsymbol{\delta}.
$$

It discards second and higher derivatives. This is accurate when parameter
shifts that matter to the result are small enough that

$$
\left|
\tfrac12\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\mathsf T}
H_i\boldsymbol{\delta}
\right|
\ll
\left|
\nu_{0,i}+(A\boldsymbol{\delta})_i
\right|
$$

for each relevant bin, where $H_i$ is the Hessian of the original bin
expectation. SGHF does not compute $H_i$; this inequality is a diagnostic
concept, not an internal test.

Practical warning signs are large nuisance pulls, a parameter at a bound,
strongly different upward and downward templates, or disagreement between
SGHF and pyhf under small finite parameter scans.

### 2. Gaussian main-measurement objective

The Poisson deviance is not used in the fit. SGHF uses squared residuals with
variance equal to a nominal or iterated expectation. The local quadratic
approximation to a Poisson log likelihood becomes better when expected counts
are sufficiently large and the fitted residuals are small relative to the
expectation. There is no universal count threshold: the relevant error also
depends on tail probability, parameter boundaries, and how far the tested
hypothesis lies from the fitted point.

Sparse bins, zero observations, far-tail discovery or exclusion, and results
controlled by a small number of bins require particular care. Poisson toy
generation can calibrate the statistic implemented by SGHF, but it does not
remove the response linearization or turn the quadratic statistic into the
original likelihood ratio.

### 3. Diagonal main variance

SGHF treats main bins as conditionally independent once the parameters are
specified:

$$
V_{ij}=0\qquad(i\ne j).
$$

Correlations induced by shared parameters are represented through $A$ and
$P$, not by off-diagonal entries in $V$. A workspace requiring an additional
bin-to-bin data covariance that is not expressible through its modifiers is
outside this model.

### 4. Gaussian auxiliary constraints

`normsys` and `histosys` parameters have unit Gaussian precision by default,
and configured Gaussian widths are represented by
$P_{jj}=1/\sigma_j^2$. `lumi` can obtain the same form from measurement
configuration.

The effective constraints for `staterror` and `shapesys` are also Gaussian in
SGHF. HistFactory represents these statistical uncertainties with
Poisson/gamma-type auxiliary terms, so this is an additional approximation.
It is most reliable when the effective auxiliary count

$$
\tau_j=P_{jj}
$$

is large enough that the corresponding constraint is close to quadratic over
the fitted range. Small $P_{jj}$, large pulls, or a lower-bound solution near
zero are warning signs.

### 5. Local asymmetric-modifier treatment

`histosys` uses the central template difference and `normsys` uses the central
derivative of pyhf's code-4 interpolation. Only that derivative survives in
$A$. Consequently, SGHF does not reproduce different positive- and
negative-direction curvature away from the expansion point.

Compare the magnitudes and shapes of the high and low variations. A large
asymmetry about the nominal template makes a single central derivative a weak
description even if the nominal value agrees exactly.

### 6. Fixed response matrix and missing cross curvature

The response matrix is evaluated once during loading. For simultaneous scale
modifiers, derivatives include the other modifiers' configured initial
factors, but later evaluation is additive:

$$
\nu_i
=
\nu_{0,i}
+
\sum_j A_{ij}(\theta_j-\theta_{0,j}).
$$

Terms such as
$(\theta_j-\theta_{0,j})(\theta_k-\theta_{0,k})$ are absent. The approximation
is therefore local in joint parameter space, not merely local one parameter
at a time.

### 7. Boundary handling

SGHF applies fixed values and parameter bounds to the quadratic problem.
This correctly minimizes the compiled objective within those bounds, but the
geometry at a boundary need not match the nonlinear likelihood geometry.
Reported covariance entries for parameters active at bounds are set to zero;
they are not one-sided confidence intervals.

The asymptotic distribution of a test statistic can also change at a
boundary. That issue is separate from the quality of the linear response and
is discussed in [hypothesis testing](hypothesis-testing.md).

### 8. Positive-yield domain

The Gaussian variance and Poisson toy generator both require positive
expectations. SGHF therefore rejects evaluated or fitted points with

$$
\nu_i(\boldsymbol{\theta})\le0
$$

or non-finite yields. Reaching this condition indicates that the linear model
has been extrapolated outside its usable domain, even if the corresponding
nonlinear HistFactory model would remain positive.

## Diagnostics that should agree

For a model in a stable local regime:

- `model.expected(model.initial)` should equal
  `model.nominal_expected`;
- small positive and negative parameter shifts should be described by the
  corresponding column of `model.response`;
- nominal and iterated fits should not move materially relative to the
  precision required by the analysis;
- fitted expectations should stay well inside the positive domain;
- nuisance pulls and `FitResult.at_bound` should not indicate extrapolation.

Agreement at the nominal point and agreement of first derivatives validate
only the local compilation. They do not validate nonlinear tails, asymptotic
coverage, or the distribution of a far-from-nominal test statistic. See
[validation and applicability](validation-and-applicability.md) for the
analysis checklist.
